**Everything in the six repositories is free and MIT.** That is not a trial, it does not expire,
and every version already published stays MIT permanently. Nothing listed as free can move behind a
paywall later.

## The split

| Area | Free | Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Screens** | All nineteen: dashboard, calendar, profile, forms, tables, two chart pages, seven UI-element pages, six shell layouts, sign in, sign up, 404 | More dashboards (analytics, CRM, SaaS) and the screens that go with them |
| **Components** | 64 component families, in all six editions | Premium blocks: billing, roles and permissions, audit log, inbox |
| **Design system** | Tokens, dark mode, six shell layouts, the icon and motion treatment, runtime theming | Nothing extra |
| **Data** | Fixtures beside the screen that uses them | The server-backed record workflow: list, detail, create, edit and delete against your own API |
| **Editions** | React, Next.js, Vue, Angular, HTML, Laravel | Svelte, and the record workflow in each |
| **Support** | Issues and discussions, in the open | Priority support and a commercial licence |

## Why anything is paid at all

Because six editions of nineteen screens is real ongoing work: keeping them in step, keeping them
current with each framework's releases, and keeping the parity checks honest. Pro funds that, and it
is additive rather than a fence. Nothing was taken out of the free tier to create it.

If you would rather support the work without needing Pro, [sponsoring](/docs/sponsor) does the same
job and costs a dollar.

## What is deliberately absent from the free tier

The searchable and multi-select dropdown variants, drag-and-drop upload, and the advanced data table.
These are **absent** rather than rendered disabled, because a control you cannot use is worse than one
you can see is not included.
